The man who helped invent the technology behind every major AI assistant is switching sides. Noam Shazeer, co-author of the [2017 Transformer paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) and co-lead of Google's Gemini models, has left Google to [join OpenAI](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/18/google-gemini-co-lead-noam-shazeer-leaves-for-openai.html) as its Lead for AI Architecture Research.

The move is a major talent win for OpenAI — and a painful loss for Google.

## Why This Matters

In 2017, Shazeer and seven colleagues at Google published "Attention Is All You Need," the paper that introduced the Transformer architecture. That single idea became the foundation for ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and essentially every modern AI model. It is not an exaggeration to say the current AI boom traces directly back to this work.

Shazeer left Google in 2022 to co-found [Character AI](/news/2026-03-14-character-ai-avatars/), a startup focused on conversational AI characters. Google wanted him back badly enough to pay $2.7 billion in 2024 to acquire parts of Character AI's team and technology, bringing Shazeer back as VP of Engineering and co-lead of Gemini.

Now, less than two years later, he is leaving again — this time for Google's biggest rival.

## What He Will Do at OpenAI

At OpenAI, Shazeer will lead AI Architecture Research, overseeing the fundamental design of the company's next generation of models. The hire comes as OpenAI prepares for its [anticipated IPO](/blog/openai-for-profit/) and continues to compete with Google and Anthropic for dominance in frontier AI.

The timing is notable. Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro is expected to launch publicly before the end of June, while OpenAI continues to build on its GPT series. Having the co-inventor of the Transformer leading architecture research gives OpenAI a significant edge in designing what comes next.

## What This Means for You

For everyday AI users, this will not change anything overnight. But talent moves at this level shape the products you will use a year from now. The AI tools that improve fastest are built by the strongest research teams — and OpenAI just got considerably stronger.

If you are interested in how the competition between [AI companies](/blog/claude-code-vs-cursor/) shapes the tools available to you, this is a story worth watching.
