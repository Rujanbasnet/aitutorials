ChatGPT just became a lot more like a personal assistant. On June 17, OpenAI rolled out [Scheduled Tasks](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10291617-tasks-in-chatgpt) — a feature that lets you ask ChatGPT to do things later, on repeat, or on autopilot.

You can now tell ChatGPT things like "Send me a weather summary every morning at 7am," "Check Hacker News every afternoon for AI stories," or "Remind me to review my project plan every Friday." It will follow through without you needing to open the app again.

## How It Works

Setting up a task is as simple as asking in natural language. ChatGPT interprets your request, creates a scheduled action, and runs it at the time you specify. You can set exact times or broader windows like "morning" or "evening."

All your tasks appear on a new dedicated Scheduled page, available on both web and mobile. From there you can view, edit, pause, or delete tasks. You can run up to 10 active tasks at once.

The feature works on Web, iOS, Android, and macOS. Windows support is expected soon.

## Who Gets It

Scheduled Tasks is in beta and available to [ChatGPT Plus, Pro, and Team subscribers](https://openai.com/chatgpt/pricing/). Free users are not included yet. Some limitations apply — you cannot use voice chats, file uploads, or Custom GPTs within scheduled tasks.

This replaces the older Pulse feature, which OpenAI says will be retired within two weeks. The new system is described as faster, more reliable, and backed by better notifications.

## What This Means for You

This is a meaningful shift in what ChatGPT actually is. It is no longer just a tool you talk to when you need something — it can now work in the background on your behalf. For anyone who uses [ChatGPT daily](/tutorials/chatgpt-guide/), scheduled tasks turn it into a lightweight automation layer: a morning briefing, a recurring research check, a weekly reminder.
