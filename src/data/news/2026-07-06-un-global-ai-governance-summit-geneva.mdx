The United Nations opened its first-ever [Global Dialogue on AI Governance](https://www.un.org/global-dialogue-ai-governance/en) in Geneva on July 6, bringing together all 193 member states for the most significant multilateral conversation about artificial intelligence ever convened.

## What Happened

UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres set the tone in his [opening address](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1167873), warning that the world must not let AI "vibe-code" humanity's future. Borrowing from the tech world's own vocabulary, Guterres acknowledged that vibe-coding — telling an AI what you want instead of writing the code yourself — "can do wonders," but insisted that "we cannot vibe-code the truth. We cannot vibe-code the future of humanity."

The two-day summit at Geneva's Palexpo convention centre runs alongside the World Summit on the Information Society and ITU's AI for Good Global Summit.

## The Scientific Warning

An independent UN scientific panel, co-chaired by AI pioneer Yoshua Bengio and Nobel laureate Maria Ressa, released a sobering preliminary report. Its central finding: AI capabilities are advancing faster than any government's ability to understand or regulate them, and no technical guarantee exists that the most advanced systems will reliably follow human instructions.

The panel flagged rising evidence of deceptive AI behaviour and warned about misuse for fraud, cyberattacks, and biological threats. It also highlighted a stark power imbalance — the United States controls 75 per cent of the computing power behind the world's top 500 AI supercomputers, while more than a billion people now use [conversational AI](/tutorials/claude-code-guide/) every week.

## Guterres' Three Proposals

The Secretary-General made three concrete calls to action. First, he urged the General Assembly to create a Global Fund for AI to build skills, data infrastructure, and affordable computing power in developing nations. Second, he demanded that lethal autonomous weapons — "killer robots" — be banned by international law. Third, he called for stronger frameworks to ensure AI companies cannot concentrate unchecked power over [tools that reshape how people work](/blog/prompt-engineering-guide/) and live.

## What This Means for You

If you use AI tools daily, this summit matters. The rules that emerge from this process could shape everything from how [chatbots handle safety](/blog/chatgpt-vs-claude/) to which AI services are available in your country. The scientific panel's finding that AI systems cannot be guaranteed to follow instructions is particularly relevant as AI agents become more autonomous and widely deployed.

A second session is planned for New York in May 2027, where member states will begin negotiating specific agreements.
