SpaceX has agreed to [acquire Cursor](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/16/spacex-spcx-cursor-acquisition-ipo.html), one of the most widely used AI coding assistants, in a $60 billion all-stock deal. The announcement came just days after SpaceX's blockbuster IPO, making it the largest acquisition of a venture-backed startup in history.

Cursor helps software developers write, edit, and review code using AI. Since launching in 2022, it has grown to over one million paying users and 50,000 corporate clients, becoming a daily tool for developers around the world.

## Why This Deal Happened

The acquisition is designed to complete the AI ecosystem that Elon Musk has been building through [xAI](/tags/ai-tools/), which SpaceX merged with earlier this year. Right now, Cursor relies on third-party models from companies like Anthropic and OpenAI to power its features. Under SpaceX, it will shift to using xAI's own Grok models, trained on the company's massive Colossus data center in Memphis, Tennessee.

SpaceX said it has already been jointly training a new AI model with Cursor that will be released in both Cursor and Grok Build, xAI's own coding agent. The goal is a vertically integrated coding experience — from the AI model to the developer tool — all under one roof.

## What Could Change for Users

The big question for Cursor's existing users is whether the tool will remain model-agnostic. Cursor's popularity was built partly on its ability to use the best available AI models, including [Claude](/tags/claude/) and GPT. If competitors pull their model access from a Musk-owned product, the tool's capabilities could narrow.

Cursor's market share has already declined from 41% in mid-2025 to around 26% in May 2026, with [Anthropic's Claude Code](/blog/claude-code-vs-cursor/) now controlling roughly half the AI coding market. This acquisition could either reverse that trend or accelerate it, depending on how the integration plays out.

## What This Means for You

If you use Cursor or any AI coding tool, this deal signals a major shift in the market. AI-assisted coding is no longer a niche — it's now a $60 billion category that the world's largest companies are fighting over.

For everyday users, the practical impact will depend on execution. A tightly integrated Cursor powered by xAI's infrastructure could become faster and more capable. But a Cursor locked into a single AI model ecosystem could lose the flexibility that made it popular in the first place.

Either way, competition in AI coding tools is intensifying — and that's good news for developers, who will have [more powerful options to choose from](/tutorials/automate-workflows-with-ai/) no matter which tool they prefer.
