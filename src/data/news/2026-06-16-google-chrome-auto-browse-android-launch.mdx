Google is about to turn your phone's browser into a personal assistant that can actually do things on the web for you.

[Gemini in Chrome on Android](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/bringing-chrome-ai-to-android/), including the new auto browse feature, is launching in late June 2026. Auto browse lets you describe a task in plain language — "find me a parking spot near the airport for next Friday" or "add everything on this grocery list to my Woolworths cart" — and Chrome handles the rest. The AI navigates websites, clicks through menus, fills in forms, and completes multi-step tasks without you touching the screen.

## How It Works

Auto browse is built on Gemini and runs as an agentic tool inside Chrome's side panel. You type or speak what you want done, and the AI takes over the browser to complete the task across the open web. It can handle things like booking appointments, updating recurring orders, comparing products, and managing reservations.

Beyond task automation, [Gemini in Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/chrome-at-io26) on Android also works as a general browsing assistant. It can summarise long articles, answer questions about page content, and connect with Google apps like Calendar, Keep, and Gmail to help you act on what you find.

The feature ships baked into Android rather than as a separate app or extension, which means it integrates deeply with the operating system.

## What You Need

There are a few requirements to keep in mind. Auto browse is rolling out first in the US on devices running Android 12 or higher with at least 4GB of RAM and the language set to English-US. The first devices to get it are the Pixel 10 and Samsung Galaxy S26, with Google targeting 200 million devices by the end of 2026.

The bigger catch: it requires a paid subscription. You need either Google AI Pro at $20 per month or AI Ultra at $250 per month. The standard free Gemini experience in Chrome does not include auto browse.

## What This Means for You

This is Google's clearest move yet toward agentic AI — software that does not just answer questions but takes action. If you spend time on repetitive web tasks like filling out forms, comparing prices, or managing bookings, auto browse could save real time.

For now, it is US-only and subscription-locked, so most users outside the US will need to wait. But the direction is clear: AI assistants are moving from conversation to action, and your browser is the first place it is happening on mobile.
