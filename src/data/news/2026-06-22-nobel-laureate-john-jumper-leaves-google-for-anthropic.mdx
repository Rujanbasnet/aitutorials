Google just lost a Nobel Prize winner to a direct competitor. John Jumper, the co-creator of AlphaFold and a 2024 Nobel laureate in Chemistry, announced he is [leaving Google DeepMind](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/19/john-jumper-to-leave-google-deepmind-for-anthropic.html) after nearly nine years to join Anthropic, the company behind Claude.

The departure comes just days after [Noam Shazeer](/news/2026-06-21-noam-shazeer-leaves-google-joins-openai/), co-inventor of the Transformer architecture, left Google for OpenAI. Together, the two exits triggered a 7% drop in Alphabet shares on Monday — the company's worst trading day in a year, erasing roughly $250 billion in market value.

## Why Jumper Matters

Jumper led the development of AlphaFold, the AI system that predicted the 3D structure of more than 200 million proteins. That breakthrough transformed biological research and drug discovery, giving scientists a tool that would have taken decades of lab work to replicate. It earned Jumper and DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis the [2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2024/summary/).

In a post on X, Jumper wrote simply: "After nearly nine years, I have decided to leave Google DeepMind and join Anthropic."

## A Pattern Emerging

Losing two researchers of this calibre in one week is historically unusual. The departures point to a broader talent war that is reshaping the AI industry. Google has invested heavily in AI — with full-year capital expenditure projections approaching $190 billion — but money alone is not enough to retain the researchers who built its most important technologies.

For Anthropic, landing a Nobel laureate is a significant statement. The company has been [expanding its focus on AI for science](https://www.anthropic.com/) and Jumper's expertise in applying AI to real-world scientific problems could accelerate that effort considerably.

## What This Means for You

If you use AI tools for work or learning, the talent behind those tools matters more than you might think. The researchers who leave one company and join another bring ideas, techniques, and expertise that eventually show up in the products you use every day.

Google's [Gemini](/tutorials/gemini-tips-tricks/), OpenAI's [ChatGPT](/start/), and Anthropic's [Claude](/tutorials/master-claude-ai/) are all built by relatively small teams of elite researchers. When those researchers move, the balance of innovation shifts with them. This week, the balance tilted toward OpenAI and Anthropic.
