You have probably noticed it by now. You ask ChatGPT or Claude a question, and instead of answering immediately, the screen shows a little "Thinking..." indicator. Sometimes for a few seconds. Sometimes for half a minute.

That is not a loading screen. Your AI is actually doing something different from its usual routine — and understanding the difference can help you get much better answers.

## Regular AI vs. "Thinking" AI

When you use an AI chatbot in its normal mode, it works a lot like autocomplete on your phone. It reads your message and predicts the most likely response, word by word, based on patterns it learned during training. It is fast because it is essentially making one continuous guess.

**Reasoning models** work differently. When you turn on "thinking" mode (or the AI activates it automatically), the model pauses and works through your question step by step before writing its response. It breaks the problem into smaller pieces, considers different angles, checks its own logic, and only then gives you an answer.

Think of it like the difference between a student blurting out an answer in class versus one who grabs scratch paper, works through the problem, then raises their hand.

## A Simple Example

Here is a famous test case. Ask a regular AI model: "How many R's are in the word strawberry?"

A standard model often answers "two" — because it is pattern-matching and the word looks simple. A reasoning model will internally go through each letter — s, t, r, a, w, b, e, r, r, y — and correctly answer "three."

That might seem trivial, but it illustrates something important. When a problem requires actually working through steps rather than guessing based on vibes, reasoning models are significantly more reliable.

## When "Thinking" Mode Actually Helps

You do not need thinking mode for everything. Here is a rough guide:

**Use thinking mode for:**

- Comparing options with multiple factors ("Should I choose plan A or plan B for my small business?")
- Math or logic problems
- Planning a multi-step project or trip
- Analysing a contract, policy, or complex document
- Any question where getting it wrong would actually matter

**Stick with regular mode for:**

- Quick factual questions ("What year was the Eiffel Tower built?")
- Writing casual emails or messages
- Brainstorming and creative writing
- Summarising articles or documents
- General conversation

The rule of thumb: if you would need to sit down and think about it yourself, your AI probably should too.

## Where to Find It

Every major AI tool now offers reasoning in some form, though they call it different things:

- **ChatGPT** lets you choose between Instant (fast, regular) and Thinking modes. On some plans, it automatically switches to thinking when your question is complex enough.
- **Claude** calls it **Extended Thinking**. You can toggle it on, and you will see a collapsible "thinking" section that shows a summary of the model's internal reasoning.
- **Gemini** has its own thinking toggle that works similarly.

On free plans, you will usually get a limited number of "thinking" messages per day. On paid plans, it draws more from your usage allowance because the AI is genuinely doing more work behind the scenes.

## What This Means for You

You do not need to understand the technical details of chain-of-thought reasoning or reinforcement learning. What matters is this: you now have two gears available to you when using AI.

**Gear one** is fast, cheap, and great for everyday tasks. **Gear two** is slower, more resource-intensive, but dramatically better at anything requiring real thought.

Most people either never use thinking mode (and wonder why AI fumbles complex questions) or use it for everything (and wonder why it is so slow). Now you know when to shift gears.

The next time you see that "Thinking..." indicator, you will know the AI is not frozen — it is doing exactly what you would do with a hard question. Taking a moment to actually work through it.
