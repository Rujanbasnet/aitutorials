If you've been using ChatGPT regularly, you might have noticed something odd this week. Your AI feels... different. Maybe its writing style shifted. Maybe it's faster but less creative. Maybe a feature you relied on just disappeared.

You're not imagining it. On June 27, OpenAI officially [retired GPT-4.5](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001051-retiring-gpt-4o-and-other-chatgpt-models) — the last model from the GPT-4 family. If you were using it, your conversations now run on a GPT-5 series model instead. And GPT-5.6, the next major upgrade, is already in limited preview.

This kind of change happens every few months. Here's what's actually going on.

## AI Models Are Like Phone Software Updates (But Bigger)

When Apple releases a new version of iOS, your phone gets new features and a slightly different look. AI model updates work similarly — except the changes go deeper.

Each model version isn't just a patch. It's a fundamentally retrained system with different strengths, weaknesses, and even personality. GPT-4.5 was widely loved for creative writing. The GPT-5 series is faster and more capable at reasoning, but some users say it feels less "warm." These aren't bugs. They're the natural result of building an entirely new model.

The key difference from a phone update: you can't choose to stay on the old version. When a model is retired, everyone moves forward.

## What Actually Changes (And What Doesn't)

When a model gets retired, here's what you need to know:

**Your conversations are safe.** All your chat history stays exactly where it is. Existing conversations continue on the new model automatically.

**Your Custom GPTs keep working.** They get migrated to the closest replacement model. You might notice subtle differences in how they respond, but they don't break.

**The AI's "personality" shifts.** This is the big one. Each model version has its own style — how it structures sentences, how creative it gets, how verbose or concise it is. The interface looks identical, but the mind behind it has changed.

**Capabilities can come and go.** New models often gain abilities (better reasoning, longer [context windows](/non-coder/what-is-a-context-window/)) but can lose edge-case behaviors you might have relied on. That trick you discovered for getting perfect cover letters? It might not work the same way anymore.

## The New Model Landscape

AI models are also getting more complex in how they're offered. OpenAI's upcoming GPT-5.6 release comes in three tiers: **Sol** (the most powerful, with advanced [agent-like abilities](/non-coder/what-are-ai-agents/)), **Terra** (balanced for everyday use), and **Luna** (fast and affordable).

This mirrors a broader trend. Instead of one model that does everything adequately, companies are offering specialised models for different tasks. Google does this with Gemini, Anthropic does it with Claude's model tiers. Choosing the right tier for your task is becoming a real skill.

## What This Means for You

The constant model churn can feel frustrating, but a few habits make it manageable:

**Don't build rigid workflows around AI quirks.** If your process depends on the AI responding in a very specific way, it will break at the next update. Build flexible workflows that work across different models.

**Try the new model before judging it.** Every model retirement brings complaints, but the new versions are genuinely more capable in most ways. Give yourself a week to adjust before deciding you hate it.

**Save prompts that work well.** When you find a prompt that gives great results, save it somewhere. If a model update changes the output, you can tweak the prompt rather than starting from scratch. Good [prompt techniques](/start/) transfer across models better than memorised tricks.

**Pay attention to [memory settings](/non-coder/ai-memory-explained/).** When models change, the AI's memory of your preferences carries over — but how it uses that memory might shift. Review your memory settings after a major model change to make sure everything still makes sense.

The AI you're talking to today won't be the same AI you're talking to in three months. That's not a flaw — it's how the technology improves. The more comfortable you get with change, the better you'll be at using whatever comes next.
